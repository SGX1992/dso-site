Dubai Silicon Oasis design

One place for the design of Dubai Silicon Oasis online. First the brand, drawn live: design DNA, logo, colour, type, pattern, effects, imagery and motion. Then the website system built on it: tokens, 42 components, 14 page templates and the rules that hold them together. District IO, the second brand, has its own tab.

The website system is generated by the same build that produces the site, from the same component code, so what you see is what is live (181 pages). The brand chapters follow the Figma brand book and are written in src/brand.mjs.

Overview

Design DNA

Six ideas explain almost every decision in the identity and on the website. If something new has to be designed and no rule covers it, these decide.

01

The connected node

The logo symbol is a row of nodes joined by lines: companies, people and places that are connected. The same shape returns as the pattern, as the pill of the navigation and as the round ends of buttons.

02

Two blues do the work

Navy Blue speaks: every headline and every paragraph. Neon Blue acts: every button, link and highlight, and the page itself when it is time to get in touch. The bright accents stay in reserve.

03

Tight headlines, calm text

Barlow Bold with letters set close together gives headlines one compact shape. Inter stays quiet for reading. IBM Plex Mono marks anything technical: counters, coordinates, labels.

AaAa01
04

Round and soft

Large radii, pills and long, low shadows tinted with Navy Blue on the website. Glass is used where text sits on a photograph.

05

The real place

Photographs and renderings of the district itself come first, all under one mild grade. Illustration appears only in the Glass style, for technology that cannot be photographed yet.

06

Things come into focus

Content arrives from blurred to sharp and glides to rest. Nothing bounces, nothing counts up, nothing loops for decoration. Depth comes from speed: what is far away moves more slowly.

Functional Icons

For functional and utility-based use cases, DSO uses the open-source Plex icon set from the Carbon Design System (IBM Design Language).

This extensive library provides a wide range of icons for interface, navigation, and wayfinding needs, such as controls, settings, and system actions.

Chosen for its cohesion with the DM Sans typeface, the icon set complements the brand’s clean, modern aesthetic while prioritising clarity, familiarity, and ease of use.

Functional icons should remain purely utilitarian and should not be modified or stylised to match brand treatments.

Motion

Motion is not part of the brand book yet. On the website it has one character: things come into focus and glide to rest. This chapter explains the rules and the curves behind them.

From blurred to sharpHeadlines arrive letter by letter and figures digit by digit, each from a soft blur into focus. Blocks rise 34 to 40 px while they fade in.
Glide to restEvery curve starts fast and ends slowly, so things settle instead of stopping. The only spring on the site is the small pull of a button under the cursor.
One after anotherGroups reveal with 80 ms between items and letters with about 34 ms. One answer is open per FAQ, one panel per menu.
Depth by speedThe hero picture moves at about two thirds of the page speed. Pictures travel 9 to 18% inside their frames, picture blocks run 20 to 70 px ahead, card columns take turns.
The page reacts to scrollThe background glides from tone to tone as sections pass, and the navigation shrinks to a pill after the hero. Scroll-linked motion has no duration of its own.
What never movesNumbers do not count up. Nothing bounces or shakes for attention. With “reduce motion” switched on, everything appears at once.

The curves

A curve describes how a movement spends its time: steep means fast, flat means slow. All curves for arriving content start steep and end flat. Press play to see the same distance travelled on each curve.

Places where the book disagrees with itself or with the website. Each needs one decision, then the book or the website is corrected.

Website system

From here on: how the website is built. Everything below is generated from the live code.

Principles

Blue is the only accentNeon Blue #006FFF carries every highlight. No orange, no teal labels, no second accent colour on any page.
Real photographsPhotography and District.io renderings only. No stock illustration, no emojis, no thumbnails inside option cards. Never grayscale: photos keep their colour.
Titles are not shoutedCard, row and stage titles use normal capitalisation. Only eyebrows and mono labels are uppercase.
No italics in the sansEmphasis is colour (the blue accent) or weight, never italic.
The wordmark is never croppedAlways the full Dubai Silicon Oasis lockup, white on dark and navy on light.
Endorsements in capitalsLines such as “BY DUBAI SILICON OASIS” are set in capitals.
Shadows are long and softTwo layers: a tight low-opacity contact shadow plus a long 80–160px blur with negative spread, tinted navy. Tokens --sh-card and --sh-float.
The pattern stays in the backgroundThe node pattern (.pat) is used at 3–6% opacity with a blurred halo. It must never compete with text. Any element carrying it clips its own overflow.
Background changes on scroll, sections stay transparentDo not give a section its own background colour. Name a tone (white, page, sand, sky, blue) and let the page background move to it. Blue is a tone, not a box. Dark photo areas stay rounded inset cards.
No splash screenEvery page, the home page included, opens directly and is scrollable from the first frame. The entrance is the navigation dropping in and the hero copy rising.
One pinned rail per pageThe horizontal rail pins the viewport. Two on one page feel like the page is stuck.
British EnglishLicence, programme, centre, organisation. Figures as DSO publishes them.
Every page ends with a way forwardInquiry form, a call-to-action band or the “next page” teaser. No dead ends.

Motion hooks src/js/dso.js

Motion is declared in the markup with data-attributes and handled once in the script. Components already carry the right hooks; this table is the reference when building something new. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion. Smooth scrolling is Lenis; scroll-linked effects are GSAP ScrollTrigger. Pinned sections are created at script time, never after the loader, or deep links land in the wrong place.

Pattern

The brand pattern (two oval nodes joined by a line, staggered rows) is a tiling SVG held in --pat-navy and --pat-white. The class .pat draws it in two layers, a sharp core and a blurred halo, masked to fade out. Position it with --pat-at and set strength with --pat-o (0.03–0.06).

Shown here at double strength so it is visible. Trap: the halo extends 40px past its element; the element (or its section) must clip overflow or phones get a horizontal scrollbar.
