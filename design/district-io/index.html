Logo

Logo

Our logo is the primary identifier for our brand. It represents who we are and sets the tone for how we are perceived. To maintain brand consistency and ensure a strong, recognisable presence, it’s important that the logo is used correctly and consistently across all applications.

Find guidance on the logo elements and usage within this section.

Overview

To ensure flexibility and consistency across all brand communications, we use three variations of our logo. Each has a specific purpose and should be applied appropriately.

Primary Logo
The primary logo is the main expression of the District IO identity and should be used wherever possible. It presents the full brand name and is the most recognisable and authoritative representation of the brand.

Logo with Tagline
This version pairs the primary logo with the District IO tagline. It is used when additional meaning or narrative is required, most commonly within campaign-led communications or brand storytelling.

Symbol
The symbol is a simplified, shorthand expression of the brand. Designed for clarity at smaller sizes and in more compact spaces, it can be used where the District IO identity is already established or where a minimal, graphic presence is required.

Primary Logo

This is the primary version of our logo and should be used in most situations. It represents our full brand name and is the most recognisable expression of our identity.

Clear space

To maintain clarity and impact, the District IO logo must always be surrounded by a minimum clear space.

This clear space is defined as the equivalent of a quarter segment taken from the “O” within the IO symbol.

Maintaining this minimum clear space ensures the logo remains legible, unobstructed, and visually distinct in all applications, preventing interference from surrounding text, imagery, or graphic elements.

Typography

Typography

Typography is a core part of how we communicate - bringing clarity, structure, and personality to everything we do.

Our type system is designed to feel contemporary and progressive, balancing simplicity with versatility, the District IO typeface supports clear communication at every scale. From wayfinding and UI to storytelling and editorial content, our typeface ensures our brand always feels considered, confident, and easy to engage with.

Primary
typeface

Plex Sans is the core typeface for District IO.

It has been selected for its clean, modern character and its flexibility across digital, print, and environmental applications. Designed for clarity and legibility at all sizes, it supports everything from functional information to expressive brand moments.

The typeface also plays an important role in the identity itself. The serifed “I” ensures the name District IO reads clearly and correctly in all contexts, reinforcing distinction and avoiding ambiguity while remaining consistent with the brand’s technical and contemporary tone.

Typographic
hierarchy

Typographic hierarchy helps structure information and guide the reader through content clearly and intuitively.

By using a considered range of type sizes, weights, and styles, District IO creates clear distinction between headings, subheadings, body copy, and supporting text.

The two styles defined here can be used or referenced when creating new editorial layouts.

Custom numbers

As part of the District IO typographic system, a custom number set has been developed to add character and distinction.

Built on the foundation of Plex Sans, the numbers retain clarity and consistency with the core typeface while drawing inspiration from the stencil-like construction of the District IO symbol.

This approach allows numbers to feel both functional and expressive, reinforcing the brand’s visual language in moments where numerals take prominence, such as wayfinding, data, and key messaging.

System font

Arial is the designated system fallback typeface for District IO.

It should be used only when the core brand typeface is not available or cannot be supported due to technical or system constraints.

Graphic language

Graphic language

The District IO graphic language is designed to be flexible, expressive, and distinctly ownable.

Built to adapt seamlessly across formats and platforms, it allows the brand to respond to a wide range of content and communication needs without losing coherence.

Its contemporary aesthetic reflects the ambition and forward momentum of the businesses found at District IO, while a clear set of core principles ensures the language remains recognisable, consistent, and uniquely District IO.

The frame device

The frame device is a key component of the District IO graphic language.

Built from the “O” of the District IO symbol, it provides a distinctive and ownable structure for content. The four segments of the form can expand and flex, allowing imagery, typography, and information to be framed, revealed, or contained in a consistent yet adaptable way.

Used across channels, the frame device brings clarity, rhythm, and recognition to layouts, reinforcing the District IO identity while supporting a wide range of content needs.

The guide shows this page with reference photographs that are not licensed for use. The frames here hold the cluster illustrations of District IO instead.

The grid

The grid is visual device derived from the frame device and acts as a foundational layout structure.

Formed from a set of four guiding lines, the grid introduces clarity, alignment, and consistency across layouts while remaining flexible enough to adapt to different formats and content types.

Beyond structure, the grid visually reinforces the precision and order associated with technology, supporting compositions that feel intentional, balanced, and distinctly District IO.

Constructing
the grid

The grid and frame device are designed to work together as a single, flexible system.

When constructing a layout, grid lines should always remain light and unobtrusive, using a stroke weight between 0.5pt and 1pt. This ensures structure is present without overpowering content or competing with the frame device.

At no point should the grid use an overly thick stroke, as this undermines the sense of precision and restraint central to the District IO visual language.

Grid lines consist of stroke weights that can range between 0.5pt and 1 pt.

Grid lines consist of 4 lines that build from the frame device.

The grid lines flex and adapt with the frame device.

Colour accents

Colour accents are used sparingly within the District IO visual system to introduce moments of vibrancy and emphasis.

Applied in small amounts, these accents help guide attention and add energy to layouts without overwhelming core brand elements.

All colour accents take the form of gradients, created using the electric accent colours from the primary palette, or white.

Robotics

Layout
considerations

District IO layouts should feel clear, considered, and precise. When combining graphic language elements with imagery and content, priority should always be given to clarity, hierarchy, and legibility. The frame device, grid, typography, and colour accents should work together to guide the eye, not compete for attention.

Use structure to create focus and rhythm. Allow space between elements, let content breathe, and apply graphic devices with intent rather than decoration.

What we do
  • Use the grid and frame device to organise content and establish hierarchy
  • Keep layouts open and uncluttered, with clear spacing between elements
  • Select imagery that feels focused, modern, and purposeful
  • Apply colour accents and gradients sparingly to highlight key moments
  • Ensure typography remains legible at all sizes and distances
What we avoid
  • Avoid overly busy or complex imagery that competes with content
  • Do not overcrowd layouts with too many graphic elements
  • Avoid using the frame device in ways that obscure imagery or reduce legibility
  • Do not scale or distort graphic elements beyond their intended proportions
  • Avoid using colour accents purely decoratively or at excessive scale

Art direction

Art direction

Art direction clarifies how District IO is expressed through imagery.

A flexible art-direction system allows the brand to communicate across a wide range of subjects while maintaining a consistent, contemporary look and feel.

Art direction
categories

District IO art direction is grounded in clarity and precision.

Across all imagery, the overall tone should feel clean, technical, and considered, supported by neutral, calm colour palettes that allow content and graphic language to work together without visual noise.

To support a wide range of narratives and applications, art direction is organised into three distinct but complementary categories.

Cluster specific photography

Full-bleed imagery used to represent the six individual District IO clusters.

Photography should feel purposeful and forward-looking, helping communicate the focus, expertise, and ambition of each cluster while remaining cohesive within the wider brand system.

Work & lifestyle photography

Imagery that captures life around District IO - from living, working, and collaborating to eating, socialising, and everyday moments.

This category brings a human layer to the brand, balancing technical ambition with approachability and authenticity.

Cut-out photography

Photography of isolated subjects, including objects and people, carefully cut out for use within the graphic language.

Subjects typically reference the six clusters - such as robotic arms, microchips, and technology components - and are used to create bold, flexible compositions that integrate seamlessly with frames, grids, and colour accents.

The guide illustrates the three categories with reference photographs that are marked “for reference only and not licensed for use”. They are not shown on this page.

Cluster specific
photography

Cluster-specific imagery should feel technical, precise, and considered. Photography is full bleed imagery that focuses on environments, processes, and objects that clearly represent the six District IO clusters.

Images should appear clean and controlled, with an emphasis on structure, detail, and modern technology. When sourcing imagery, prioritise visuals that feel contemporary and purposeful, avoiding overly stylised or abstract treatments.

  • Stock libraries: Select high-quality imagery with clean compositions, neutral or controlled colour palettes, and a clear technological focus. Avoid busy backgrounds or excessive visual effects.
  • Photoshoots: Where possible, commission bespoke photography to capture real processes, equipment, and environments. Shoots should be carefully art-directed to maintain consistency in lighting, tone, and composition.

Work & lifestyle
photography

Work and lifestyle photography captures the everyday experience of life around District IO.

Imagery should reflect the warmth and light of Dubai, the greenery of landscaped terraces, and the modernity of work environments, balancing architecture, people, and place.

Photography should feel natural, contemporary, and human - showing moments of working, meeting, living, eating, and socialising. Subjects should appear authentic and unposed.

  • Stock libraries: Choose imagery with warm, natural light, calm colour palettes, and a modern, aspirational feel. Avoid overly stylised, generic, or stock-heavy compositions.
  • Photoshoots: Where possible, commission bespoke shoots to capture real spaces, people, and moments across the campus. Focus on consistency in lighting, tone, and composition.

Cut-out
photography

Cut-out photography should feature a single, clearly defined subject, selected to feel technical, modern, and relevant to the District IO clusters. Subjects may include objects, components, or people.

All cut-out imagery should be shot using a consistent studio-style lighting approach, ensuring clarity and cohesion across the system. Lighting should be soft and controlled, with even exposure and minimal shadowing.

  • Stock libraries: Select imagery with clean backgrounds, centred subjects, and neutral lighting that can be easily isolated. Avoid images with complex edges, strong shadows, or dramatic lighting effects.
  • Photoshoots: Where possible, commission bespoke studio shoots to ensure consistent lighting, perspective, and scale across subjects.

Motion

Motion

Motion strengthens the clarity, energy and precision to our District IO brand system.

It supports meaning, reinforces structure, and adds rhythm to how elements appear, move and resolve across touch points.

This section outlines the principles, behaviors and techniques that define our motion system.

Animated logo

Our logo moves with intent. The motion is snappy, magnetic, and exact. Movements build with clear acceleration and deceleration, reaching a central peak in speed.

This concept is clearly reflected in our lockup animation. Each element snaps into place with precision with each movement feeling guided and controlled.

We have two reveal styles for the IO shorthand: a long form and a short form.

Long form
The long form emphasises the six sections of District IO and is best used in moments where the full structure can be seen and understood.

Short form
The short form version is more direct. It’s still made of the six part system, but with more pace. It can be used in short content or faster transitions where time is limited.

The animations themselves are in the Figma file of the guide (present or preview the page to play them). They are shown here as stills.

Easing

To create motion that feels magnetic, snappy and exact, we use steep easing curves stretched across generous frame durations. This gives each movement space to build and resolve smoothly.

We aim for balance by using the same easing value for both in and out. This keeps the acceleration and deceleration evenly weighted, placing the peak speed at the centre of the movement.

Frame Device

The four sections of our frame device always remain equal in size and can move apart along the horizontal and vertical axes, either independently or at the same time. One section is never larger than another.

Once expanded on a plane, the device can rotate around either the horizontal or vertical axis, but never both simultaneously. This prevents diagonal or off-axis rotation, preserving the structural clarity of the frame.

No matter the movement, each section always faces the camera.

Context examples

When pairing content with our frame device, we treat imagery and typography differently.

We aim to introduce content through frame rotation rather than scaling from the centre. This allows for a more natural, spatial reveal.

Image content

For imagery, we use a masking system. The mask rotates in 3D space with the frame, while the image stays flat. This preserves the original perspective of the photography and avoids distortion. The result feels like a window into the world of District IO, not a spinning flat image.

Text content

For type, we allow the text to rotate with the frame. Masking type would cause awkward clipping of its characters, reducing clarity. Letting it follow the frame’s perspective reinforces a sense of physicality and momentum.

Icons & illustration

Icons & illustration

Icons and illustrations play a supporting but essential role within the District IO brand system.

We use a combination of a bespoke icon style, and an extensive library of pre-existing icons that work seamlessly together.

Overview

District IO uses a tiered icon and illustration system to ensure clarity, consistency, and appropriate levels of expression across all touchpoints.

Each tier has a distinct role, allowing the brand to scale from expressive storytelling to simple functional communication without losing coherence.

Tier 1 (200px+)

Illustration

Brand illustrations are large-scale, expressive visuals used as hero storytelling devices.

They are typically themed around the six District IO clusters and broader technology narratives. These illustrations play a leading role within layouts and are used sparingly for maximum impact.

Tier 2 (20-80px)

Brand icons

Brand icons are mid-scale graphic elements used to support content and navigation.

Simplified in form, they draw directly from the stencil-inspired visual language of the District IO symbol, ensuring they feel distinctive and ownable while remaining clear and versatile.

Tier 3 (10-40px)

Functional icons

Functional icons are practical, small use, utility-led symbols used for everyday interface and wayfinding needs.

These are sourced from a consistent, open-source icon library and are used for common actions and UI elements, prioritising clarity and familiarity over brand expression.

Illustration

We use two distinct illustration styles across our brand. Both serve a different tone, allowing us to adapt across a range of moments while maintaining consistency.

Illustration style 1: Mono
This more monochrome, photo-real approach reinforces the precision, clarity and engineered finish of District IO. Used in moments where focus and function are key.

Illustration style 2: Glass
A more contextual, colour-infused style. It leans into our brand palette, introducing a broader tonal range. These illustrations tend to fill the frame with spatial context. Ideal for storytelling, flexibility, and creative-led moments where character and atmosphere matter.

Prompting

To generate illustration assets that stay consistent with the District IO brand, we use a structured three part prompt system.

Master Prompt
Defines the global rules including lighting, geometry, surface treatment, and tone.

Material Section
Outlines the exact materials to be used and how they behave.

Object Description
Applies the above parameters to a specific subject. It describes the object’s shape, function, scale, and key design details.

By stacking these three sections into a single prompt, we create a modular and editable system.

Base Prompt
Purpose:

Establishes the global visual rules across all assets. This defines how objects are presented rather than what they are.

Includes:
  • Framing and composition
  • Lighting style and shadow behaviour
  • Scene background and environment
  • Realism level and rendering quality
  • Visual tone and atmosphere
  • Global constraints (no branding, no people, no text)
Key phrases:
  • High-fidelity CGI
  • Minimalist and function-first
  • Controlled light and soft shadow
  • Clean background or seamless backdrop
  • No visual clutter or branding
  • Balanced exposure and focus
  • Floating or lightly grounded objects
Material Prompt
Purpose:

Defines what materials are used and how they behave under light. This ensures visual realism and cohesion across scenes.

Includes:
  • Primary and secondary material types
  • Surface treatment and reflectivity
  • Detail realism and part junction logic
  • Texture condition and cleanliness
  • Assembly credibility
Key phrases:
  • Soft satin polymers or semi-gloss plastics
  • Lightly brushed or polished metal
  • Visible seams or surface joins
  • Clean, well-maintained surfaces
  • Subtle edge lighting
  • Controlled use of reflections
Object Prompt
Purpose:

Adapts the style to different objects without breaking consistency.

Includes:
  • Object category, function, and key features
  • Shape, scale, and detail logic
  • Pose and orientation
  • Constraints or context as needed
Key phrases:
  • Matte or satin polymer primary surfaces
  • Brushed or bead-blasted secondary components
  • Clear part separations and logical assembly
  • Visible seams, joints, and fasteners
  • Clean, factory-fresh surface condition
  • Subtle edge highlights to reveal form
  • Minimal use of high-gloss finishes

Brand icons

Brand icons are custom-designed graphic elements created for specific District IO use cases.

Tier 3
Functional icons

For functional and utility-based use cases, District IO uses the open-source Plex icon set from the Carbon Design System (IBM Design Language).

This extensive library provides a wide range of icons for interface, navigation, and wayfinding needs, such as controls, settings, and system actions.

Chosen for its cohesion with the Plex Sans typeface, the icon set complements the brand’s clean, modern aesthetic while prioritising clarity, familiarity, and ease of use. Functional icons should remain purely utilitarian and should not be modified or stylised to match brand icon treatments.

Wayfinding

Wayfinding

The District IO wayfinding system is an extension to the brand experience.

The system is designed to feel distinctive, intuitive, and considered, helping people move through the campus with confidence while reinforcing a clear sense of place.

Built from the core elements of the District IO brand language, the wayfinding system translates graphic, typographic, and spatial principles into the physical environment.

Wayfinding
system

The District IO wayfinding system is built from the core shapes of the brand symbol.

Elements derived from the IO form are used consistently across signage, markers, and navigational devices, creating a distinctive and ownable system that is immediately recognisable across the campus.

By extending the symbol into physical space, wayfinding becomes an integrated part of the wider brand language.

Wayfinding
system

District IO wayfinding uses a combination of distinctive brand elements to create clear, intuitive navigation across the campus.

Custom numbers are used at larger scales for hero signage, providing strong visual markers that are easy to recognise from a distance and reinforce the identity of place.

Brand icons are used to support navigation and orientation. Drawn from the District IO icon tier, these icons are ownable and distinctive, helping key destinations and functions stand out while remaining consistent with the wider visual language.

Raised and
Recessed

Within wayfinding, the District IO symbol is applied in two distinct physical treatments: recessed and raised.

Shapes taken from the symbol are either recessed into architectural surfaces or formed as raised, three-dimensional elements.

These forms act as containers for wayfinding content such as directional information, destinations, and floor details. Using the symbol in this way creates a flexible and adaptable system that can respond to different spaces and scales.

Sections in use

Single sections of the symbol as wayfinding elements: a lit arc for a level number, recessed and raised signs, an arc as a room marker, the two halves of the O as a sign.
